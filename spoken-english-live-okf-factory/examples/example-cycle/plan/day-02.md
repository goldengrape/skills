# Day 2 — Explain a Small Problem

## Language objective
Explain what went wrong, why, what you did, and the result.

## Topic mode
adaptive

## Planned topic or evergreen fallback
Fallback: a small everyday problem the learner solved.

## Topic adaptation card

- topic_intent: test_possible
- evergreen_fallback: a small everyday problem the learner solved
- load_reduction_fallback: use a familiar minor problem and provide one cause-effect connector
- switch_trigger: after one simpler follow-up, switch if the learner still has no accessible content or asks to change
- recent_topic_limit: do not repeat the same broad topic used in the previous session unless the learner requests it

Choose in order:

1. the learner's choice today;
2. saved next-topic recommendation;
3. technology or cooking if still relevant;
4. the evergreen fallback.

If technology and cooking are equally useful, offer those two choices only. Confirm objective fit, consent, accessible knowledge load, evidence basis, recent-topic balance, and time fit. Preserve cause–response–result language after selection.

## Scenario and roles
The coach is a curious colleague or friend asking for an explanation.

## Time budget
15 minutes.

## Recall cue
Retrieve one connector from Day 1.

## Opening question
Can you tell me about a small problem you solved in this area?

## Main speaking task
Give the sequence, cause, response, and result.

## Optional live micro-correction targets
“Because of,” “so,” “in the end,” and natural wording.

## Learner-output target and coach-turn limit
Learner speaks for roughly two-thirds or more. Coach turns stay short.

## Fatigue or short-time fallback
Use the fallback topic, ask one concrete question, make at most one repair, then closeout.

## Repair and repetition
Restate the clearest two-sentence version.

## Transfer question
How would you explain the same problem to someone unfamiliar with the topic?

## Interest evidence to capture
Record explicit preference, expanded speech, learner questions, or request to continue. Also classify whether weak participation came from topic affinity, language load, background knowledge, prompt/task quality, fatigue/time, privacy/sensitivity, or remains unknown. One positive affinity signal remains possible unless explicitly confirmed.

## Language evidence to capture
Connector retrieval and audience adaptation.

## Closeout instruction
Produce the session record, interest update, next-topic recommendation, and one next action.
