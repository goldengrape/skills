# Day 1 — How I Learn Now

## Language objective

Describe a current learning routine in a clear sequence and explain why one part works or does not work.

## Topic mode

`anchored` · intent: `anchored_practice`

## Planned topic or evergreen fallback

Planned: how you currently learn something as an adult. Fallback: how you learned a practical skill in the past.

## Topic adaptation card

- selection rule: use the planned topic; switch to the fallback if the learner has no current routine or prefers not to discuss it
- topic-fit checks: personal and safe; no specialist knowledge; supports sequence and reasons; not in recent history; setup under one minute
- evergreen fallback: learning a practical skill in the past
- load-reduction fallback: describe only three steps and one reason
- switch trigger: no usable personal example after one simpler prompt
- preserve after switching: keep the sequence-and-reason objective

## Scenario and roles

The coach is a curious new learning partner. The learner explains one real learning routine so the coach could try it tomorrow.

## Time budget

- 0:00–1:00 goal, energy, and correction controls
- 1:00–3:00 warm-up
- 3:00–10:00 main explanation and follow-ups
- 10:00–13:00 repair, repetition, and transfer
- 13:00–15:00 spoken recap and record
- optional 5-minute extension: answer one deeper follow-up; do not add another main task

## Recall cue

No prior phrase. Use: `First... Then... After that... The reason is...`

## Opening question

“What is one thing you are learning now, and what does a normal study session look like?”

## Main speaking task

Give a 60-second explanation of the routine. The coach asks up to three short follow-ups: what happens first, what is difficult, and what the learner would change.

## Optional live micro-correction targets

Prioritize blocked meaning, natural sequence markers, and one high-value word-choice or tense issue. Do not diagnose recurring errors from this first sample.

## Learner-output target and coach-turn limit

Learner: at least three connected responses and one 45–60 second turn. Coach: normally one question or one brief cue per turn; target learner speaking share at least two-thirds.

## Fatigue or short-time fallback

Give three sentences: what you learn, how you practice, and what you will do next. Repair one sentence, repeat it once, then close.

## Repair and repetition

Select one sentence that would be clearer with a sequence marker or reason clause. Give a 3–8 second correction and ask for one natural repetition.

## Transfer question

“If a friend copied your routine tomorrow, what should they keep and what should they change?”

## Interest evidence to capture

Record any explicit topic preference separately from answer length. Note whether short speech came from language load, unclear prompting, fatigue, privacy, background knowledge, or unknown cause.

## Language evidence to capture

Record baseline connected-speech length, prompting need, one strong expression, and at most one candidate pattern for later retest.

## Closeout instruction

Ask the learner for a 20-second spoken summary. Complete the session record and state patch, then set exactly one next action: retrieve one useful sequence phrase on Day 2.
