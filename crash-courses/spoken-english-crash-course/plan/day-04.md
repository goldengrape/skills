# Day 4 — Adaptive Interest Transfer

## Language objective

Explain the benefits and limits of something, support the explanation with one concrete example, and answer a follow-up.

## Topic mode

`adaptive` · intent: `test_possible`

## Planned topic or evergreen fallback

Candidates: an AI tool the learner has tried, or one challenge in adult continuing education. Evergreen fallback: a useful app and one limitation it has.

## Topic adaptation card

- candidate evidence sources: today's explicit choice, canonical next-topic recommendation, AI/adult-education signals from Days 1–3, recent three-topic history
- six checks: fits benefits/limits; learner consents; knowledge is personal or easily accessible; tests a possible interest; avoids needless recent repetition; setup fits one minute
- two-choice behavior: if no candidate is clearly better, ask “AI tools or adult learning challenges?” once
- evergreen fallback: a useful app and one limitation
- load-reduction fallback: discuss one benefit and one limit using a personal example
- switch trigger: one simplified prompt still produces a knowledge block, privacy concern, or explicit wish to change
- preserve after switching: continue to elicit benefit, limit, example, and follow-up
- recent-topic limit: if AI filled both Days 2 and 3 and the learner has no explicit preference today, prefer adult continuing education or the fallback

## Scenario and roles

The learner explains a useful but imperfect tool or learning approach to a thoughtful colleague. The coach asks for evidence and one limitation.

## Time budget

- 0:00–1:00 select topic and confirm energy
- 1:00–3:00 retrieve one trade-off phrase
- 3:00–10:00 explanation, example, and follow-up
- 10:00–13:00 repair and topic-preserving transfer
- 13:00–15:00 recap and record
- optional 5-minute extension: add a second user profile, not a second topic

## Recall cue

Use one phrase from Day 3: `One advantage is...`, `The main limitation is...`, or `It depends on...`.

## Opening question

After topic selection: “What is genuinely useful about it, and where does it fall short?”

## Main speaking task

Give a 60–90 second explanation with one benefit, one limit, and one concrete example. Answer up to three short follow-ups without changing the topic unless the switch rule applies.

## Optional live micro-correction targets

Benefit/limitation collocations, example markers, hedging, and one high-value recurring candidate from prior evidence.

## Learner-output target and coach-turn limit

Learner: one extended answer, one example, and two follow-ups. Coach: at most two choices at selection, then one short question or cue per turn.

## Fatigue or short-time fallback

Use the fallback topic. State one benefit, one limit, and one example; repair one phrase and close.

## Repair and repetition

Improve one sentence that is vague, absolute, or unnatural. Ask the learner to reuse the improved phrase in a new example.

## Transfer question

“Who would benefit most from it, and who might need a different option?”

## Interest evidence to capture

Record selection basis and direct affinity signals. Keep topic status unchanged when engagement can be explained by language load, knowledge load, prompt mismatch, fatigue, or privacy.

## Language evidence to capture

Record organization of benefit/limit/example, follow-up responsiveness, phrase retrieval, and uptake of the repair.

## Closeout instruction

Ask one meta-question only if evidence remains unclear: “Would you like to use this topic again?” Complete the record and state patch, then set exactly one next action based on language evidence, not topic preference alone.
