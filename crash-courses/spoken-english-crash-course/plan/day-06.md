# Day 6 — Adaptive Variety and Problem Solving

## Language objective

Diagnose a practical problem, propose two steps, and explain why the solution fits the person's constraints.

## Topic mode

`adaptive` · intent: `refresh_variety`

## Planned topic or evergreen fallback

Choose an underused safe topic outside the most repeated broad category. Evergreen fallback: building a healthy study habit when time and energy vary.

## Topic adaptation card

- candidate evidence sources: explicit choice today, next-topic recommendation, possible interests not used in the recent window, or a safe variety topic from daily life, travel, hobbies, or habits
- six checks: supports problem/solution speech; learner consents; answerable from experience; has a clear variety purpose; does not repeat the last three topics unnecessarily; setup under one minute
- two-choice behavior: offer at most two options, such as “a study-habit problem or planning a short trip?”
- evergreen fallback: building a healthy study habit
- load-reduction fallback: coach gives the problem—only 20 minutes available and low evening energy—and learner proposes two steps
- switch trigger: privacy, weak knowledge, or one failed simplified prompt; do not switch merely for a vocabulary block before simplifying
- preserve after switching: keep problem, two-step solution, and constraint-based reason
- recent-topic limit: avoid AI unless the learner explicitly requests it and the recent window permits useful variation

## Scenario and roles

The coach brings a practical problem. The learner acts as a helpful peer and proposes a realistic two-step solution.

## Time budget

- 0:00–1:00 select topic and confirm task
- 1:00–3:00 retrieve one recommendation phrase
- 3:00–10:00 diagnose, solve, and answer challenges
- 10:00–13:00 repair and new-constraint transfer
- 13:00–15:00 recap and record
- optional 5-minute extension: compare the solution with one alternative

## Recall cue

Use: `The first thing I would do is...`, `That would help because...`, `If that doesn't work...`.

## Opening question

After selection: “What is the real problem here, and what would you do first?”

## Main speaking task

Ask one diagnostic question, state the problem in your own words, propose two steps, and respond to one constraint introduced by the coach.

## Optional live micro-correction targets

Problem/solution signposts, conditional language, concise questions, and one high-value candidate pattern from the week.

## Learner-output target and coach-turn limit

Learner: one diagnostic question, one 60–90 second solution, and one adjustment. Coach: one short constraint and one follow-up; learner speaking share at least two-thirds.

## Fatigue or short-time fallback

Use the study-habit fallback. Give one problem sentence and two solution sentences; repair one, repeat, and close.

## Repair and repetition

Repair one vague step or unnatural recommendation. Introduce a changed constraint and ask the learner to reuse the improved structure.

## Transfer question

“What would you change if the person had more motivation but less time?”

## Interest evidence to capture

Record whether a new topic produced explicit preference, spontaneous personal examples, questions, or requested continuation. Separate novelty effects and task ease from affinity.

## Language evidence to capture

Record diagnostic question quality, solution sequence, constraint response, phrase retrieval, and repair uptake.

## Closeout instruction

Ask for a 20-second final two-step solution. Complete the record and state patch, then set exactly one next action: prepare to combine the week's three functions on Day 7.
