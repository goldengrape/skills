# Day 2 — Explain an AI Learning Workflow

## Language objective

Explain a process clearly enough for another person to follow, using sequence, purpose, and one caution.

## Topic mode

`anchored` · intent: `anchored_practice`

## Planned topic or evergreen fallback

Planned: how to use an AI assistant for learning. Fallback: how to use any digital tool for learning.

## Topic adaptation card

- selection rule: use the planned AI topic; use the fallback if the learner lacks experience or does not want AI today
- topic-fit checks: supports process language; learner may speak from experience or invent a simple method; no sensitive data; first AI session; setup under one minute
- evergreen fallback: a three-step workflow with a dictionary, video app, or note app
- load-reduction fallback: coach supplies three neutral stages—ask, check, practice—and learner explains their purpose
- switch trigger: background knowledge prevents process speech after one simplified prompt
- preserve after switching: keep sequence, purpose, and caution

## Scenario and roles

The learner is showing a classmate how to use one tool for a 15-minute study session. The coach is the classmate and asks practical questions.

## Time budget

- 0:00–1:00 confirm task and energy
- 1:00–3:00 retrieve one Day 1 sequence phrase
- 3:00–10:00 workflow explanation and questions
- 10:00–13:00 repair and teach-back
- 13:00–15:00 recap and record
- optional 5-minute extension: add one safety or quality-check step

## Recall cue

Retrieve one phrase from Day 1. New frame: `I use it to...`, `Before I trust the answer, I...`, `The final step is...`.

## Opening question

“If a friend had only 15 minutes, how would you tell them to use AI for one useful learning task?”

## Main speaking task

Explain a three- or four-step workflow. Include the purpose of each step and one way to check the result. The coach asks no more than three short clarification questions.

## Optional live micro-correction targets

Sequence markers, infinitive purpose clauses, and natural tool verbs such as `ask`, `check`, `revise`, and `practice`.

## Learner-output target and coach-turn limit

Learner: one 60–90 second explanation plus three follow-up answers. Coach: no more than two short sentences per turn and no more than three questions during the main task.

## Fatigue or short-time fallback

Explain only `ask → check → practice`, repair one sentence, and give a final 15-second recommendation.

## Repair and repetition

Choose the highest-value unclear instruction. Offer a short replacement, then ask the learner to repeat the step and continue to the next one.

## Transfer question

“How would this workflow change for someone who does not trust AI answers?”

## Interest evidence to capture

Record explicit enthusiasm, learner-initiated examples, questions, or a request to continue as topic-affinity evidence. Record vocabulary or knowledge difficulty separately.

## Language evidence to capture

Record whether the process was actionable, one successfully retrieved phrase, and one repair candidate only if it materially affected clarity or naturalness.

## Closeout instruction

Ask for a 20-second spoken version of the workflow. Save the record and state patch, then set exactly one next action: reuse one process phrase while comparing options on Day 3.
