# Day 7 — Review Conversation

## Language objective

Combine process explanation, comparison, and a qualified recommendation in one conversation with reduced scaffolding.

## Topic mode

`adaptive` · intent: `review_transfer`

## Planned topic or evergreen fallback

Choose the least-repeated suitable topic that allows review of all three functions. Evergreen fallback: design a realistic 30-day plan for learning one skill.

## Topic adaptation card

- candidate evidence sources: today's explicit choice, next-topic recommendation, week's recorded affinity evidence, recent three-topic history, and language items needing retest
- six checks: elicits all three functions; respects consent; uses accessible knowledge; has evidence for review; improves topic balance; setup under one minute
- two-choice behavior: offer at most two options drawn from an evidenced interest and the fallback
- evergreen fallback: a 30-day adult learning plan
- load-reduction fallback: make a seven-day plan with two options and one recommendation
- switch trigger: chosen topic cannot support all three functions after one concrete reframing
- preserve after switching: retain explanation, comparison, and qualified recommendation
- recent-topic limit: prefer the less-used suitable topic unless the learner explicitly requests a thematic review

## Scenario and roles

The learner helps a friend create a learning plan. The coach asks how it works, presents an alternative, and asks for a final recommendation.

## Time budget

- 0:00–1:00 choose topic and confirm review goal
- 1:00–3:00 retrieve two useful expressions from the week
- 3:00–10:00 integrated conversation
- 10:00–13:00 repair and independent retry
- 13:00–15:00 spoken review, record, and next-cycle evidence
- optional 5-minute extension: one new learner profile or constraint

## Recall cue

Retrieve two phrases without reading full examples: one sequence phrase and one comparison/recommendation phrase.

## Opening question

After topic selection: “What would a realistic plan look like, and why would it suit this person?”

## Main speaking task

Explain a short plan, compare it with one alternative, and make a qualified recommendation. Answer one challenge and revise one part without coach-provided sentence frames unless needed.

## Optional live micro-correction targets

Prioritize one item with evidence from at least two sessions or one high-impact exception. Include naturalness or register when useful; avoid unsupported pronunciation diagnosis.

## Learner-output target and coach-turn limit

Learner: two extended turns totaling at least two minutes, two follow-ups, and one independent reuse after repair. Coach: one question at a time, one challenge, minimal scaffolding.

## Fatigue or short-time fallback

Explain a three-step seven-day plan, compare two study formats in one sentence, give one conditional recommendation, repair once, and close.

## Repair and repetition

Select one evidence-backed high-value issue. Cue self-repair or give a short replacement, then ask the learner to repeat the full recommendation independently.

## Transfer question

“What would you keep if the person had only half as much time?”

## Interest evidence to capture

Ask which topic or scenario the learner wants next week only after the speaking task. Combine this explicit answer with session evidence; do not infer preference solely from fluency.

## Language evidence to capture

Record independent retrieval, connected-speech length, comparison and conditional recommendation, repair uptake, and the single highest-priority target for cycle 2.

## Closeout instruction

Complete the session record and state patch. Fill `review/cycle-review.md` and `review/next-cycle-proposal.md`, then set exactly one next action: generate cycle 2 from the completed evidence.
