# Day 3 — AI Support or Human Teacher?

## Language objective

Compare two options, name a trade-off, and state a qualified preference instead of an absolute claim.

## Topic mode

`anchored` · intent: `anchored_practice`

## Planned topic or evergreen fallback

Planned: AI learning support versus a human teacher. Fallback: self-study versus a live class.

## Topic adaptation card

- selection rule: use the planned comparison; use the fallback if AI knowledge would dominate the task
- topic-fit checks: directly elicits comparison and hedging; safe; can be answered from general experience; second planned AI use; setup under one minute
- evergreen fallback: self-study versus a live class
- load-reduction fallback: compare only cost, feedback, and flexibility
- switch trigger: the learner spends more time seeking facts than expressing a comparison
- preserve after switching: keep trade-offs and a conditional preference

## Scenario and roles

The learner advises a friend choosing support for a three-month learning goal. The coach challenges one point politely.

## Time budget

- 0:00–1:00 confirm task
- 1:00–3:00 retrieve one process phrase and introduce comparison frames
- 3:00–10:00 comparison and challenge
- 10:00–13:00 repair and revised recommendation
- 13:00–15:00 recap and record
- optional 5-minute extension: reverse the recommendation for a different learner

## Recall cue

Retrieve one phrase from Days 1–2. Use: `Compared with...`, `One advantage is...`, `The main trade-off is...`, `It depends on...`.

## Opening question

“For an adult learner, when is AI support more useful, and when is a human teacher more useful?”

## Main speaking task

Give a 60–90 second comparison using two criteria. State a preference for one learner profile. Respond to the coach's challenge: “What might change your recommendation?”

## Optional live micro-correction targets

Comparatives, `on the one hand / on the other hand` when natural, and hedging with `may`, `might`, `tends to`, or `depends on`.

## Learner-output target and coach-turn limit

Learner: one extended comparison, two follow-ups, and one revised recommendation. Coach: one question at a time, one challenge only, and brief corrections.

## Fatigue or short-time fallback

Name one benefit of each option, say `It depends on...`, make one recommendation, repair and repeat one sentence, then close.

## Repair and repetition

Choose one overgeneralized or unclear comparison. Give a short hedged alternative and ask the learner to reuse it in the recommendation.

## Transfer question

“Would your answer change for learning a language rather than learning software? Why?”

## Interest evidence to capture

Do not count fluent answers caused by strong background knowledge as interest by themselves. Record explicit choice, spontaneous examples, questions, or requested continuation separately.

## Language evidence to capture

Record comparison structure, hedging accuracy, response to challenge, retrieval of an earlier phrase, and any repeated candidate pattern with its prior evidence reference.

## Closeout instruction

Ask for a 20-second qualified recommendation. Complete the record and state patch, then set exactly one next action: use one trade-off phrase in Day 4's selected topic.
