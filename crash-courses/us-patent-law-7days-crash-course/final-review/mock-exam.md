## Part A — Terms, 20 points

Explain four of the following in 80–120 words each:

1. right to exclude
2. PHOSITA
3. anticipation
4. enablement
5. claim construction
6. IPR
7. judicial exception
8. written description

## Part B — Comparison, 15 points

Choose one:

1. Compare novelty and nonobviousness.
2. Compare written description and enablement.
3. Compare utility patent and design patent.
4. Compare literal infringement and doctrine of equivalents.

## Part C — Issue Spotting, 30 points

A company develops a software tool that uses a known statistical method on ordinary user data to predict equipment failure. The claim recites collecting data, applying the statistical method on a conventional server, and sending an alert. A prior patent disclosed collecting the same data and sending alerts, but did not disclose this exact statistical method. A journal article disclosed the statistical method in a different maintenance context. The specification gives one working example and then claims all uses of the method for all industrial equipment.

Analyze the main patentability issues under §101, §102, §103, and §112. Then briefly state how claim construction would matter if the patent later went into litigation.

## Part D — Source and Update Control, 10 points

You are told: “The MPEP says X, so a court must rule X.” Explain why that is too strong. Then list two current-source checks you would perform before teaching or relying on a modern USPTO policy topic.
