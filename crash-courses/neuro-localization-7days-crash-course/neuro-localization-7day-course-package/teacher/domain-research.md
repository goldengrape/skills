## Domain Findings

1. Neurologic reasoning should first identify the anatomic location of the lesion, then pathophysiology, then differential diagnosis.
2. The neurologic examination provides evidence categories needed for localization: cognition, cranial nerves, motor, sensory, cerebellar, gait, reflexes and long-tract signs.
3. Localization training should use cases, because single signs are often ambiguous until combined.
4. Peripheral localization needs pattern discrimination: root, plexus, single nerve, multifocal nerve, polyneuropathy, NMJ, muscle.
